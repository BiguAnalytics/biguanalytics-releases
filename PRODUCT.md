# Product

## Register

product

## Users

BiguAnalytics is used by the Bigua Rugby Club technical staff on a Windows desktop machine. The primary user is the match analyst, who tags rugby events while watching a local MP4 or YouTube video. The secondary user is the head coach, who reviews dashboards, PDFs, clips, and tactical material after the match.

The usage context is task-heavy: live or post-match analysis, time pressure, dense sports data, and repeated workflows where the interface should stay out of the analyst's way.

## Product Purpose

BiguAnalytics replaces the club's external LongoMatch and HLR reporting workflow with an internal offline-first rugby analysis tool. It lets the club tag match events, review statistics, export presentable PDFs, generate video clips, and keep local ownership of match data.

Success means the analyst can complete the flow from video loading to tagging, dashboard review, PDF export, and clip export faster and with less friction than the current external process.

## Brand Personality

Precise, technical, and professional. The product should feel like a serious analysis tool for a rugby staff, not a marketing site or a decorative dashboard.

The interface should communicate authority and control, while remaining fast enough for live tagging and clear enough for coaches reviewing reports.

## Anti-references

Do not make the app look like a generic SaaS landing page, a decorative glassmorphism showcase, or a colorful consumer sports app. Avoid overloaded chrome during tagging, ornamental animations, large marketing-style cards, and visual effects that compete with the video or match data.

LongoMatch is a functional reference for tagging and timeline behavior, not a visual reference. HLR-style reports are a reporting reference, but the desktop app should remain a focused product interface.

## Design Principles

1. Minimal UI during capture, maximum clarity during analysis.
2. Preserve local-first trust: video, clips, PDFs, and match JSON stay on the user's machine unless an explicit optional sync path is configured.
3. Make defaults operationally useful so coaches and analysts do not need to configure templates or filters before getting value.
4. Prefer dense, scannable information over decorative layout.
5. Use consistent controls, states, and terminology across tagging, dashboard, PDF, clips, heatmap, and tactical board flows.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

The product should maintain WCAG 2.1 AA intent for contrast, visible focus, keyboard operation, and non-color-only state communication. All clickable controls need explicit affordance and pointer cursor. Motion should be short, purposeful, and safe for reduced-motion users.
